# Interview: Sourcer

- Role: Sourcer
- Office: Dubai
- Date: 6 October 2026
- Interviewer: Riklr FDE

---

**Sourcer:** Hi. Sorry, is it okay if I keep my laptop open? I have a search running for Doha and the recruiter wants names by lunch.

**Interviewer:** Of course. Thanks for talking to me. What does a sourcer do here?

**Sourcer:** I find candidates for the recruiters. I do not own roles. A recruiter sends me a job description and says "find me ten." I search the professional network, the job boards and our own database. I put the good ones into the ATS against the role and the recruiter takes it from there.

**Interviewer:** How long have you been doing it?

**Sourcer:** About a year and a half. I joined after university. I studied psychology, actually. I thought I would go into HR at a big company. Then a friend said agencies hire fast and you learn everything. That part was true.

**Interviewer:** How many roles do you support at once?

**Sourcer:** It depends who is shouting loudest. Usually eight to twelve. I work mostly for three recruiters in Dubai and sometimes Doha. Riyadh has their own sourcers but sometimes they send me overflow when their seniors are drowning. Then I have fifteen and I just do whatever has the angriest message.

**Interviewer:** How do you keep track of fifteen?

**Sourcer:** My own spreadsheet. Role, who asked, what I searched, who I messaged, who replied. The ATS does not show me who I already contacted, so without the sheet I would message the same person twice. I also keep a notes file with my search strings for each type of role. Warehouse, hospitality front office, finance. I copy and change them. Some recruiters ask me for the file.

**Interviewer:** Where do you search first?

**Sourcer:** Our database. It is free and the candidates already know us. But it is messy. You search for a name and you get the same person three times. Different spellings, different emails. Mohamed, Mohammed, Muhammad. Al Mansoori, Almansoori, Mansoori. Sometimes first name and last name are swapped because the CV had the family name first. Sometimes the name is only in Arabic and I cannot search it at all.

**Interviewer:** Can you show me one?

**Sourcer:** Sure. *[Typing.]* Here. This is a hotel revenue manager I found last month for the Doha hotel. One record from 2021, Khalid with a K-H-A-L-I-D, a personal email. Another from this year, Khaled with an E, a work email. Same phone number in the CV file. Different current employer, because he moved. The old one says he is still at the first hotel. If I had taken the old one, I would have told the recruiter he is at the wrong company.

**Interviewer:** How do you decide which record is right?

**Sourcer:** I look at the phone number in the CV file, the employer history, the photo if there is one. It takes time. Usually I just pick the newest one. If I miss that it is a duplicate, the same person ends up attached to the role twice.

**Interviewer:** Has that happened to you?

**Sourcer:** Once that I know of. The recruiter caught it before it went out. She was not happy. She said, "Check before you attach." I said I did check. The names were different. Not just a spelling. One had his middle name and one did not.

**Interviewer:** How big is the database?

**Sourcer:** The Managing Partner says twenty-five thousand. I think a lot of those are the same people. And a lot are old. People who applied in 2019 for a role and never came back.

**Interviewer:** Do you contact those older candidates?

**Sourcer:** Sometimes. If the profile is good I message them. Some reply "how do you have my CV?" because they do not remember applying. One person asked me to delete their data. I did not know how, so I asked compliance.

**Interviewer:** Is there a flag in the ATS that says whether you can contact someone?

**Sourcer:** There is a consent field. A lot of the old records have it blank. Nobody told me what to do with blank. I assumed it was fine because they are in our system.

**Interviewer:** Was there a training on it?

**Sourcer:** I think there was one before I joined. Or maybe in my first month, when I was on leave for my sister's wedding. I am not sure. Nobody gave me a document.

**Interviewer:** What about the job boards? Do you search those?

**Sourcer:** Yes. And the inbound applications from the boards come into the ATS automatically. That is the flood the recruiters talk about. I look at those too for some roles. Honestly, most are easy to throw out. Someone in Manila applying for a Riyadh role that needs local experience and a transferable visa. Someone with one year of experience for a manager role. You could set rules for most of that.

**Interviewer:** Why does nobody set those rules?

**Sourcer:** The boards have screening questions, but the recruiters do not always set them up. And the job descriptions are vague. If the job description does not say how many years or which visa, what do you filter on?

**Interviewer:** How much of your day is reading CVs versus searching?

**Sourcer:** Reading is maybe a third. Searching and messaging is most of it. And fixing records. Merging duplicates by hand when I find them, though I am not sure I am allowed to merge.

*[Her laptop pings.]*

**Sourcer:** Sorry. The Doha recruiter. "Any names?" I told her twelve o'clock. It is ten forty. *[Laughs.]* Okay.

**Interviewer:** Is it always like that?

**Sourcer:** With some recruiters. The busy ones are stressed, so I get it. They carry so many roles they cannot remember what they asked me. Sometimes two recruiters ask me for the same profile for different clients, and I have to decide who gets the good candidate. That feels wrong. I am the most junior person in the room.

**Interviewer:** You mentioned Arabic CVs. What do you do with those?

**Sourcer:** I do not read Arabic well. I can read slowly, I grew up here, but not a full CV in a hurry. If the CV is in Arabic and the parser did not fill the fields, I usually skip it. If it is mixed, English and Arabic, I can read the English part. I know that is not great. Some of the best candidates for Saudi roles probably sit in there.

**Interviewer:** Do recruiters give you feedback on who you find?

**Sourcer:** Sometimes. If a candidate gets shortlisted I see the stage change. If the client rejects the whole shortlist I usually do not hear why. I just get a message: "need more, different profile." I find out later it was because they wanted nationals only. If someone had told me at the start, I would have searched differently.

**Interviewer:** When did that last happen?

**Sourcer:** Two weeks ago. A Riyadh overflow role, a route planner. I sent nine names, mostly Indian and Egyptian, strong profiles. Then the message: "need more, different profile." I asked the recruiter's colleague in the office. She told me, "They want Saudi only. Everybody knows that client." I did not know. I am in Dubai.

**Interviewer:** How do you know who is a national?

**Sourcer:** The nationality field. But it is free text. "Saudi," "KSA," "Saudi Arabian," sometimes in Arabic. Some are blank. I search a few versions. I have them saved in my notes file. For Emirati it is worse. "UAE," "Emirati," "UAE National," "local."

**Interviewer:** What do you think about the idea of AI screening CVs?

**Sourcer:** It depends what it is trained on. If it learns from who the recruiters shortlisted before, it learns their habits. I have seen some of those habits. For warehouse and site roles, the recruiters almost never shortlist women, even when the CV is good. I am not sure the client even asked for that. So if a machine copies that, it is worse.

**Interviewer:** Have you raised that with anyone?

**Sourcer:** Not formally. I am junior. I mentioned it once to my recruiter and she said the client prefers men for site roles. I do not know if that is true. I did not ask which client. Maybe I should have.

**Interviewer:** What would help you most?

**Sourcer:** One clean record per person. A clear rule on which old records we can use. And knowing the real requirements on day one, including nationality. Then I can find the right people the first time.

**Interviewer:** Thank you. That is very helpful.

**Sourcer:** Thank you for asking. Nobody usually asks us. Now I have about an hour to find twelve hotel people in Doha. Yalla.
