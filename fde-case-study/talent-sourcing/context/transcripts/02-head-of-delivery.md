# Interview: Head of Delivery

- Role: Head of Delivery
- Office: Dubai
- Date: 5 October 2026
- Interviewer: Riklr FDE

---

**Head of Delivery:** Give me one second, I am just closing the SLA sheet for the week. Every Monday I tell myself I will finish it before ten. Every Monday it is eleven.

**Interviewer:** No rush.

**Head of Delivery:** Okay. Done. Go ahead.

**Interviewer:** The Managing Partner said you have the numbers. Can you walk me through how you track delivery?

**Head of Delivery:** I have a spreadsheet. Every Monday each recruiter updates their tab with their open roles and the date the shortlist went out. I pull it together and colour it. Green if we hit the SLA, red if we did not. It is mostly red, as I am sure he told you.

**Interviewer:** He said he reads the summary line.

**Head of Delivery:** *[Laughs.]* That sounds right. The summary line is the polite version.

**Interviewer:** How reliable is the sheet?

**Head of Delivery:** It is as good as what people put in it. Some recruiters update it properly. Some update it on Monday morning from memory. And most of them keep their own tracker as well, their own spreadsheet with their own columns, and they copy from that into mine. So there are two versions of every role and they do not always agree. The ATS has stage dates, but people move candidates through stages in batches, so those dates are not perfect either. If you want the truth, the ATS is closer than my sheet.

**Interviewer:** Where do you think the time goes?

**Head of Delivery:** Honestly, a few places. I am not sure screening is the biggest. I know that is what the Managing Partner believes. Recruiters do complain about CV volume. But when I look at a role that took four weeks, it is rarely four weeks of reading CVs.

**Interviewer:** What is it then?

**Head of Delivery:** Two things I see a lot. First, the intake. A hiring manager calls, says "I need a warehouse supervisor, same as last time." The recruiter writes it up from the old job description. Nobody asks about salary band, nationality, who is on the interview panel. Then we send a shortlist and the client says "too expensive" or "we need a Saudi." That is a full round wasted.

**Interviewer:** And the second?

**Head of Delivery:** Waiting for clients. We send a shortlist and then we chase. And chase. Some hiring managers reply the same day. Some take two weeks. In the meantime the good candidates take other offers. Then when the client finally says yes to someone, that person is gone and we start again.

**Interviewer:** Can you show me a role where that happened?

**Head of Delivery:** Let me find one. *[Scrolling.]* Here. Warehouse supervisor for the logistics group, Dammam site. Opened in February. First shortlist went out, I think, around day twelve. Five candidates. HR came back after nearly two weeks and said they needed Saudis for that grade. Nobody had written that down. Second shortlist, three Saudis, two expats. Then the hiring manager said the role now covers Dammam and the new Jubail site, so he wants someone who has run two sites. That was not in the brief either. Third shortlist was accepted. By then it was May.

**Interviewer:** Why so long between rounds?

**Head of Delivery:** Part of it was Ramadan. It fell right in the middle. Working hours are shorter, hiring managers are not at their desks in the afternoon, everyone is tired. Then Eid, and half the managers take the full week. Nothing moves. Every year my sheet goes completely red from Ramadan to the end of Eid, and again in summer when people travel. We know it is coming and we still promise the same SLA.

**Interviewer:** Does the SLA say anything about client feedback?

**Head of Delivery:** It does, actually. There is a line that says the client will give feedback within three working days. Nobody enforces it. We would never stop the clock on a client. The Managing Partner would not allow it.

**Interviewer:** Have you asked him?

**Head of Delivery:** Twice. The answer was, "We are a service business." Which I understand. He brings the clients in. He has to keep them happy. But he also signs things I then have to deliver. The logistics group renewal last year, he agreed seven working days in the room. I found out from the contract.

**Interviewer:** Is the SLA clock from when the role opens?

**Head of Delivery:** From when the role is confirmed. Which in practice we take as the day the hiring manager emails us. Some recruiters start counting from the day the job description is approved, which makes their numbers look better. I have told them not to.

**Interviewer:** Let us talk about the intake form. Who fills it?

**Head of Delivery:** The recruiter who owns the role. In theory during the call. In practice after, sometimes days after. Half the forms I see have empty fields. The nationality field is the worst. Recruiters leave it blank because they assume "open" or they assume the client will say. And the form itself has gaps. It does not ask about nationalisation directly. It just says "nationality preference," which sounds optional.

**Interviewer:** When was the form last updated?

**Head of Delivery:** Version three. 2023, I think. I wanted to change it this year and then we had the Doha restructure and it slipped. A few recruiters made their own versions. One of the Riyadh seniors has a page of extra questions he stapled to the back. Good questions, actually.

**Interviewer:** How do you know the nationality issue costs you rounds?

**Head of Delivery:** I do not have a number. It is a feeling from reading client emails. When a Saudi client rejects a shortlist, there is a good chance it is about Saudisation. They just do not always say it clearly. Sometimes they say "not the right fit" and you find out later.

**Interviewer:** What about the Riyadh logistics group? The Managing Partner described them as demanding.

**Head of Delivery:** That is polite. They are our biggest account and our hardest. The intake is usually fine on paper. Then the hiring manager changes his mind. Or a different hiring manager takes over. Or they decide the role now covers two sites, like the one I just showed you. We have had roles go four rounds. My recruiters in Riyadh are exhausted by that account.

**Interviewer:** Does anyone track why shortlists get rejected?

**Head of Delivery:** There is a dropdown in the ATS for the reason. Recruiters fill it maybe half the time. The client comment usually gets pasted in from the email, so the real reason is in there if you read it.

*[Her phone buzzes. She reads it.]*

**Head of Delivery:** Sorry. Doha. A candidate accepted an offer and then told us his current employer will not release him for ninety days. The client thought it was thirty. Nobody asked him properly. Okay, that can wait ten minutes.

**Interviewer:** Does that happen often?

**Head of Delivery:** Notice periods, visa transfers, yes. A candidate says "immediate" and it turns out he means after his bonus in March. Or his visa is tied to his employer and the transfer takes weeks. We find out late because nobody asks early.

**Interviewer:** How is work allocated across recruiters?

**Head of Delivery:** Each office lead allocates. In theory by sector and capacity. In practice, the client asks for a specific recruiter and they get it. Or the senior recruiter who has the relationship takes it. So yes, a few people carry a lot. I have two in Riyadh who have each had more than seventy roles in the last twelve months. Most recruiters have fifteen or twenty. That is not sustainable.

**Interviewer:** The Managing Partner thought load was fairly even.

**Head of Delivery:** It is not. I have told him. He sees the people he talks to, and they are the busiest. One of those two in Riyadh has not taken a proper holiday since last summer. I am worried I lose him. Then I lose half the logistics account with him.

**Interviewer:** Do you think AI CV screening would help?

**Head of Delivery:** It would help a bit with the reading. I do not think it fixes the three weeks. And I worry about it. If it gets a Saudi CV wrong because it is in Arabic, or ranks people by something unfair, that is our name on it. Also, half of what the recruiters reject is obvious. Wrong country, no visa, not enough years. You do not need AI for that, you need the job board filters set up properly.

**Interviewer:** Do sourcers help?

**Head of Delivery:** Yes, but there are only six of them. They mostly search the network and our own database. The database is a mess. The same person can be in there three times under three spellings.

**Interviewer:** If you could fix one thing, what would it be?

**Head of Delivery:** A proper intake. If we got the requirements right on day one, I think half the rejections go away. And then something that makes clients reply faster. A nudge, a deadline, anything. But I have said that before and I may be wrong. I am going on what I see in the emails, not on data.

**Interviewer:** Thank you. That is clear.

**Head of Delivery:** Talk to the Riyadh team. They live it more than I do. And if you can, get me the real numbers from the ATS. I would like to know if my feeling is right.
