# Interview: Client HR Business Partner

- Role: HR Business Partner at the Riyadh-based logistics group (client)
- Office: Client head office, Riyadh
- Date: 7 October 2026
- Interviewer: Riklr FDE

---

**HR Business Partner:** Welcome, welcome. Sorry for the wait at reception. Security here is very strict since we moved buildings. Please, have some gahwa. And dates, they are from Qassim, very good.

**Interviewer:** Thank you. Thank you for speaking with us. You work with the firm on behalf of your company. Can you describe your role?

**HR Business Partner:** I support the operations division. Warehousing, fleet, transport planning, customs. Around four thousand people. When a hiring manager needs someone, I approve the headcount and then it goes to the recruitment partner. The firm is our main partner for mid and senior roles. For junior roles we use our own team and the national employment platform.

**Interviewer:** How long have you worked with the firm?

**HR Business Partner:** Me personally, three years. The company, longer. The relationship started at the top, between our CEO and their Managing Partner. So when I joined, the firm was already there. I did not choose them. But I have no problem with them.

**Interviewer:** How would you describe the service?

**HR Business Partner:** The people are nice. The relationship at the top is strong. But the shortlists are not good enough. I would say half the time the first shortlist is not usable.

**Interviewer:** What makes a shortlist unusable?

**HR Business Partner:** Many things. The most common is nationality. We have Saudisation targets by grade and by department. Our compliance team tracks it monthly. If I am below target in operations, I cannot approve an expat hire for a supervisor role. Full stop. So when the firm sends me four expats and one Saudi, I have to reject it.

**Interviewer:** Does the firm know the target for each role?

**HR Business Partner:** They should. It is obvious. We are a Saudi company. Most of our grades below manager are reserved for nationals now.

**Interviewer:** Is it written in the role request?

**HR Business Partner:** The hiring manager raises the request. He talks to the recruiter. He may not mention it because he is thinking about the job, not the quota. I am not always on that call. Maybe I should be.

**Interviewer:** Can you think of a recent example?

**HR Business Partner:** A warehouse supervisor in Dammam, earlier this year. The manager liked one candidate very much. Egyptian, very experienced, he had run a bigger warehouse than ours. The manager called me directly and said "I want this one." I had to say no. Operations was under target that month. He was angry with me, not with the firm. He said HR is blocking the business. That is the part people do not see. The firm sends the shortlist, and the fight happens inside our company.

**Interviewer:** How did it end?

**HR Business Partner:** We waited for another shortlist. The manager complained to his director. His director complained to my director. In the end we hired a Saudi from the third round, I think. A good hire. But everybody was upset for two months.

**Interviewer:** What else makes a shortlist unusable?

**HR Business Partner:** Fit. The candidates do not match what the manager needs.

**Interviewer:** Do the requirements change after the intake?

**HR Business Partner:** Sometimes. We are a growing business. We open a new site, the scope of a role grows. A manager moves and the new manager has his own view. That is business. A good recruitment partner should adapt.

**Interviewer:** How often does that happen?

**HR Business Partner:** I would not say often. Maybe a few times a year.

**Interviewer:** Is that a number you track?

**HR Business Partner:** No. It is my impression. I do not see every conversation between the manager and the recruiter.

*[A colleague knocks and puts a folder on the desk. A short exchange in Arabic.]*

**HR Business Partner:** Sorry. Offer letters for signature. Our month end. Please continue.

**Interviewer:** How fast does your side give feedback on a shortlist?

**HR Business Partner:** Two or three days. We are quick.

**Interviewer:** The recruiters mentioned they sometimes wait longer.

**HR Business Partner:** Maybe for some managers. Our managers travel between Riyadh, Dammam and Jeddah. Some are on the warehouse floor all day. They do not sit at email. And I need to review for Saudisation before it goes to the manager, so there are two steps. But generally we are quick.

**Interviewer:** Is there any time of year when it is slower?

**HR Business Partner:** Ramadan, of course. Hours are shorter. People work late at night, not in the morning. Then Eid, and many managers go home to their families for the full holiday. Summer also, people travel. But that is the same for every company in the Kingdom. Everybody knows it. Outside those times, we are quick.

**Interviewer:** Who reviews first, you or the hiring manager?

**HR Business Partner:** It depends. Sometimes the firm sends it to the manager directly and copies me. Then the manager likes someone, and I have to say no because of nationality. That creates friction inside our company too. Like the Dammam case.

**Interviewer:** Would a different process help?

**HR Business Partner:** If the firm checked nationality against our targets before sending, it would save everyone time. I would even share our grade rules with them. Nobody has asked for that.

**Interviewer:** What would that look like? Is it a document?

**HR Business Partner:** A table. Grade, department, the share we need to keep for nationals. It changes when our numbers move, so I would update it every quarter. I could send it with every role. It is not secret. It is just that nobody asked.

**Interviewer:** Do you see visa or notice issues with expat candidates?

**HR Business Partner:** Yes. Even when we can take an expat, for a specialist role, the transfer takes time. And many come from companies with ninety days notice. The manager hears "ninety days" at final interview and he is upset because he needed the person last month. That should be asked at the start, not at the end.

**Interviewer:** How do you measure the firm's performance?

**HR Business Partner:** Time to hire and quality of hire. We do a quarterly review. Last quarter I told them we were not happy with speed. Another firm sent us a shortlist for a fleet supervisor in eight days.

**Interviewer:** Was that shortlist accepted?

**HR Business Partner:** It was a good shortlist. All Saudi. We hired from it.

**Interviewer:** Does that firm get more roles now?

**HR Business Partner:** Some. I cannot say more. Our procurement team likes to have two partners anyway. But I would prefer that the firm improves. They know our business. A new partner takes a year to learn us.

**Interviewer:** What do you think about recruiters using AI to screen CVs?

**HR Business Partner:** I do not mind how they do it. I want good candidates fast. But I would be careful. We had a vendor in another area use an automated tool and it rejected a lot of Saudi graduates because their CVs were in Arabic. That became a problem with our management. If the firm uses something like that, I want to know how it works.

**Interviewer:** Is there a preference for gender in your operations roles?

**HR Business Partner:** No. We have women supervisors in two of our warehouses now. It is a priority for the company actually. We want more women in operations. I would like to see more in the shortlists.

**Interviewer:** That is interesting. Have you told the firm?

**HR Business Partner:** I have said it in quarterly reviews. I am not sure it reaches the recruiters. The quarterly review is with the Managing Partner and sometimes the Head of Delivery. The recruiters are not in the room.

**Interviewer:** If you could change one thing about the process, what would it be?

**HR Business Partner:** A proper brief at the start, with me on it, covering nationality, salary and what the manager really needs. And a shortlist that respects it. Then we can move fast.

**Interviewer:** And on your side? Anything your company could change?

**HR Business Partner:** Maybe we could be clearer when a role changes. Sometimes the manager tells the recruiter on the messaging app and I do not even know. It is not perfect on our side either. Our managers are operations people. They like to pick up the phone, not fill forms.

**Interviewer:** Thank you. That is very candid.

**HR Business Partner:** You are welcome. I want this to work. They are a good partner when it goes right. Please, take some dates with you. They will go to waste here.
