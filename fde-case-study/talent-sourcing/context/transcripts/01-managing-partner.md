# Interview: Managing Partner

- Role: Managing Partner
- Office: Dubai
- Date: 5 October 2026
- Interviewer: Riklr FDE

---

**Managing Partner:** Sorry, sorry. I was on with Riyadh. Please, sit. You want coffee? Tea? They make good karak downstairs.

**Interviewer:** I am fine, thank you. Thanks for making the time. Can you start with why you brought us in?

**Managing Partner:** Sure. We are a good firm. Fourteen years, three offices, clients who have been with us a long time. But the market has changed. Clients want shortlists faster, and they have options now. Two of our clients told me this year that another firm got them candidates in a week. One of them is using AI to screen, they say it on their website. I do not want to be the firm that is slow and old fashioned.

**Interviewer:** Which two clients?

**Managing Partner:** One is the logistics group in Riyadh. The other is the fintech here in Dubai. The fintech I understand, they are young, they like anything with AI in the name. The logistics group worried me more. That is a serious account.

**Interviewer:** What does slow look like for you today?

**Managing Partner:** Our contracts say 7 to 10 working days to a shortlist. Honestly, we are closer to three weeks. My Head of Delivery has a sheet every Monday and it is mostly red. I just read her summary line.

**Interviewer:** Where do you think the three weeks go?

**Managing Partner:** Screening. No question. You post a role for a logistics manager in Riyadh and you get two hundred, three hundred applications. Half of them are not even in the country. The recruiter has to open every CV, read it, decide. That is days of work per role. And my recruiters are carrying a lot of roles. They tell me every week they are drowning in CVs.

**Interviewer:** When did you last see that yourself? A specific role.

**Managing Partner:** Last month. A warehouse operations manager for the logistics group. I walked past one of the Riyadh desks on my visit and the recruiter had the applicant list open. Hundreds. She was scrolling and scrolling. I remember thinking, this is a person with a degree doing the work of a filter. And I get CVs too, you know. People send them to me on my phone. Friends of friends, someone's cousin who is a great finance manager. I forward them to the team by email. I probably send ten a week.

**Interviewer:** Do those go into the ATS?

**Managing Partner:** I assume so. I send them to the recruiter. What happens after, you would have to ask them.

**Interviewer:** Is the volume across all roles or some roles?

**Managing Partner:** Most roles. Maybe tech is a bit better because the applicants are more targeted. But hospitality, logistics, construction, the volume is huge.

**Interviewer:** And what do you want AI to do?

**Managing Partner:** Read every CV the moment it comes in. Score it against the job description. Give the recruiter a ranked list, top twenty, with a reason for each. The recruiter calls the top ten, sends five to the client. I think that takes a week out of the process. Maybe more.

**Interviewer:** Have you looked at any tools?

**Managing Partner:** A few vendors have pitched us. The ATS we use has an add-on as well, but it does not read Arabic CVs properly. That matters for us. Riyadh is our fastest growing office and a lot of Saudi candidates send Arabic CVs or both languages.

**Interviewer:** When did Riyadh open?

**Managing Partner:** 2018. No, 2019. We signed the lease in 2018, the team started in 2019. Doha came in 2022. Riyadh is where the growth is. Every company there is hiring.

*[His phone rings. He looks at it.]*

**Managing Partner:** I have to take this, it is the logistics group. Two minutes.

*[He steps to the window. The call is partly in Arabic.]*

**Managing Partner:** Ahlan, ya habibi. Yes. Yes, I saw the email. No, no, we will have people for you this week, inshallah. I will speak to the team myself. Of course. Salam.

*[He comes back.]*

**Managing Partner:** Sorry. That was their COO. He wants a shortlist for a regional fleet role and he wants it yesterday. You see what I mean. This is the pressure every day.

**Interviewer:** Did he say anything about what the role needs?

**Managing Partner:** He said "the same as the last one, but stronger." The team will sort the details. Where were we?

**Interviewer:** The client side. What happens once a shortlist goes out?

**Managing Partner:** The client reviews it, picks who they want to interview. Most of the time it goes well. I would say seventy, seventy-five percent of our shortlists get accepted. When they do not, it is usually because the candidates were not strong enough. Which again comes back to screening.

**Interviewer:** Where does the seventy to seventy-five come from?

**Managing Partner:** From experience. From what the office leads tell me in our monthly call. Nobody has given me a report on it, if that is what you mean. Maybe you can.

**Interviewer:** How quickly do clients come back?

**Managing Partner:** Depends on the client. Most within a few days. Some are slower. Our biggest client, the logistics group in Riyadh, they are busy people. They run a huge operation. But they are our most important account, so we work around them.

**Interviewer:** How big is that account?

**Managing Partner:** About a fifth of our roles. Maybe a quarter of fees. I personally own that relationship. Their CEO and I go back a long way.

**Interviewer:** Do they accept most of what you send?

**Managing Partner:** They are demanding. They know what they want. Sometimes we need two rounds. That is normal for a client that size.

**Interviewer:** Does the delivery team ever push back on what you agree with them?

**Managing Partner:** *[Laughs.]* My Head of Delivery would say yes. When we renewed their contract I gave them seven working days. She was not happy. But you do not walk into that boardroom and offer ten days when the other firm is offering a week. Sales sets the promise, delivery keeps it. That is the job.

**Interviewer:** You mentioned Saudi candidates. How does nationalisation affect your work?

**Managing Partner:** It is a big part of the Saudi market. Every client there has Saudisation targets. The recruiters know this. It is in the brief when it matters.

**Interviewer:** Is it always in the brief?

**Managing Partner:** It should be. I would be surprised if it was not. Our recruiters are experienced. In the UAE it is the same story with Emiratisation, the targets go up every year. Everyone in this industry lives with it.

**Interviewer:** What about the intake call with the hiring manager? How does that work?

**Managing Partner:** The recruiter calls the hiring manager, gets the requirements, writes the job description. We have a form for it. It is not complicated. The good recruiters do it in thirty minutes.

**Interviewer:** Have you sat on one recently?

**Managing Partner:** Not recently. I used to do all of them myself. Now I do the relationship side. If a CEO calls me I make the introduction and the team picks it up.

**Interviewer:** If AI screening worked, what would success look like in six months?

**Managing Partner:** Shortlist inside ten working days on most roles. Acceptance rate up. And recruiters handling more roles each without burning out. Right now I cannot hire fast enough, especially in Riyadh. Good recruiters are expensive. I lost two this year to in-house jobs. The big clients hire our people directly and pay them more.

**Interviewer:** How is work spread across the recruiters today?

**Managing Partner:** Fairly evenly, I think. Each office manages its own allocation. Some of my senior people take more because they have the client relationships. That is how it should be. The client wants a face they trust. If the logistics group asks for a particular recruiter, they get that recruiter.

**Interviewer:** Is there anything you would not want AI to do?

**Managing Partner:** I do not want it talking to clients. And I do not want it making the final call on who gets shortlisted. But ranking and filtering, yes. If it filters out the obvious no's automatically, even better. Why should a person read a CV from someone with two years of experience for a role that needs ten?

**Interviewer:** Are there any concerns from your side on candidate data?

**Managing Partner:** Our compliance officer will tell you more. She is very careful, which is her job. We have a big database, more than twenty-five thousand candidates. That is an asset. I would like the AI to search it as well. Half the time the right person is already in our system and nobody remembers them.

**Interviewer:** Has that happened? The right person was already in the system.

**Managing Partner:** Many times. A finance director for the Abu Dhabi bank two years ago. We searched for six weeks. Then the candidate told us in the final interview that he had applied to us in 2017. He was in our database the whole time. We just did not find him.

**Interviewer:** Last question. If you had to bet, what is the one thing slowing you down most?

**Managing Partner:** Volume of CVs. Recruiters spending their day reading instead of talking to candidates and clients. Fix that and the rest follows.

**Interviewer:** Thank you. That is very helpful.

**Managing Partner:** Talk to my Head of Delivery next. She has the numbers. And she will tell you I am wrong about half of this, so you will get both sides. Yalla, I have the Doha call at four.
