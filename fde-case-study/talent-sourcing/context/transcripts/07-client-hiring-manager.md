# Interview: Client Hiring Manager

- Role: Operations Manager, Eastern Province sites, at the Riyadh-based logistics group (client)
- Office: Client regional office, Dammam
- Date: 8 October 2026
- Interviewer: Riklr FDE

---

**Hiring Manager:** Sorry, sorry. I am in the car between Dammam and Jubail. Is the sound okay? If I go quiet it is the tunnel near the port.

**Interviewer:** The sound is fine. Thanks for making time on the road.

**Hiring Manager:** The road is the only time I have. In the office there is always someone at the door. Truck late, forklift broken, customs holding a container. Go ahead.

**Interviewer:** Can you describe your role?

**Hiring Manager:** I run operations for our Eastern Province sites. Two warehouses in Dammam, the new site in Jubail, and the transport teams that serve them. Around nine hundred people, with drivers. When I need a supervisor or a planner or a manager, I raise it and the recruitment partner works on it.

**Interviewer:** How many roles have you had with the firm this year?

**Hiring Manager:** Many. Let me think. Supervisors, transport planners, a fleet manager, a customs coordinator. Maybe ten, maybe twelve. Jubail opened in spring, so this year was heavy.

**Interviewer:** How does a role start on your side?

**Hiring Manager:** I message the recruiter. The senior one in Riyadh, he knows us well. I say, "I need a warehouse supervisor." He calls me, we talk for ten, fifteen minutes. He asks the questions. Then he sends me a job description and I approve it.

**Interviewer:** Do you read the job description closely?

**Hiring Manager:** *[Laughs.]* Honestly? I read the first part. If the title and the main duties are right, I say okay. It is two pages. I have forty people waiting for decisions. I trust him to get it right.

**Interviewer:** What happens when the first shortlist comes?

**Hiring Manager:** This is the important part. On paper they all look fine. Ten years, logistics, good companies. Then I look closer and I think, this man has run a warehouse for retail. Small boxes, shelves. My warehouse is heavy goods, containers, cold chain. It is a different job. The CV does not tell you that. So I say no, and I explain what I really need.

**Interviewer:** Was that explained at the intake?

**Hiring Manager:** Some of it. But you do not always know what you need until you see who is available. When I see five people, I understand the market better. Then I know what to ask for. The brief evolves. That is normal, no?

**Interviewer:** Can you give me a recent example?

**Hiring Manager:** This week. A transport supervisor. The shortlist came on Sunday. Five people. I looked at it on Monday night at home. Good people. But two of our biggest contracts now go through the port, so the supervisor must understand customs paperwork. I called the recruiter on Tuesday and said, "Add customs." He was not happy, I could hear it. But that is the reality on the ground.

**Interviewer:** Did you put that in writing?

**Hiring Manager:** I told him on the phone. Maybe I sent a voice note after. He knows. Why do I need to send a letter for one line?

**Interviewer:** Did HR know about the change?

**Hiring Manager:** I do not think so. HR approves the headcount. The details of the job are between me and the recruiter.

**Interviewer:** Have other roles changed like that?

**Hiring Manager:** Some. The Dammam warehouse supervisor this year. When Jubail opened, I needed the same person to cover both sites for six months. So the role became bigger. You cannot hold a business still because a job description was signed in February. Wallah, if I waited for paperwork every time, nothing would move.

**Interviewer:** How often would you say your roles change after the intake?

**Hiring Manager:** Not often. Sometimes. The big changes, maybe two or three this year. Small changes, like adding customs, I do not count. That is just giving better information.

**Interviewer:** Tell me about the Dammam role. I heard there was a candidate you liked.

**Hiring Manager:** *[Sighs.]* Yes. An Egyptian supervisor. Very strong. He had run a bigger site than ours, heavy goods, exactly what I needed. I wanted him the same day. Then HR said no. Saudisation. Operations was under target that month.

**Interviewer:** Did you know about the target when you raised the role?

**Hiring Manager:** I know we have targets. Everybody knows. But which grade, which percentage, this month or next month, that is HR's job. They have the numbers. I am an operations man. I need someone who can run a shift at three in the morning when the cold store alarm goes. If HR has rules, HR should tell the recruiter. Not me.

**Interviewer:** How did you feel about how it went?

**Hiring Manager:** Angry, to be honest. I told my director that HR is blocking the business. Later I understood their side. But for me it was two more months with a gap on the night shift. My shift leaders covered it. They were tired. One of them resigned in the summer.

**Interviewer:** Let us talk about feedback. When a shortlist arrives, how quickly do you respond?

**Hiring Manager:** When I get time. I try to look in a few days. But you must understand my week. Let me give you an example. In August we had the peak before the school season, all the retail clients stocking up. The shortlist for a transport planner came. I saw the email. I flagged it. Then I did not open my inbox properly for, I think, ten days. Maybe more. When I opened it, the recruiter had chased three times. One candidate had already taken another job.

**Interviewer:** Does the recruiter chase you in other ways?

**Hiring Manager:** On the messaging app. Every two days. "Any feedback, ya ustaz?" I know, I know. I reply "inshallah tomorrow." And I mean it. Then tomorrow there is a truck accident or an audit. It is not that I do not care. I care a lot. These people will work for me.

**Interviewer:** Does anyone else need to review before you give feedback?

**Hiring Manager:** Sometimes I wait for my director if it is a manager grade. And HR looks at nationality. Sometimes HR looks first, sometimes I look first. It depends who the recruiter sends it to. I prefer he sends it to me first. I know the job.

**Interviewer:** HR told us they usually give feedback in two or three days.

**Hiring Manager:** HR, maybe. HR sits at a desk. *[Laughs.]* I am not saying they are wrong. For their part, they are quick. But a shortlist is not finished when HR says okay. It needs me. And I am on the road.

**Interviewer:** What would help you respond faster?

**Hiring Manager:** Fewer CVs, better ones. If he sends me three people who are right, I will reply in one day. If he sends five and I need to read every CV to find the problem, it waits. Also a short summary. One line each. Salary, notice, can he do nights, has he done cold chain. I do not have time for five PDF files.

**Interviewer:** Do you see notice periods and visas as a problem?

**Hiring Manager:** Sometimes. A candidate says he can join in a month. Then at the offer we find ninety days. Or for an expat, the transfer takes longer than anyone said. For me this is the worst, because I already planned the shift.

**Interviewer:** How do you see the firm overall?

**Hiring Manager:** They are good people. The senior recruiter in Riyadh works very hard. I know he has many roles, not only mine. But sometimes I feel they send what is easy to find, not what the site needs. The other firm we tried this year sent a shortlist in eight days. All Saudi. They sat with me for an hour first. One hour. It made a difference.

**Interviewer:** What did they ask in that hour?

**Hiring Manager:** Everything. Shifts, what goes wrong on a bad night, who the person replaces, salary, nationality. They called HR themselves. I did not have to explain the grade rules. Maybe that is the difference. I do not know.

**Interviewer:** What do you think about recruiters using AI to read CVs?

**Hiring Manager:** I do not care how they read them. I care who arrives at my door. If a machine can tell me who has done cold chain at night, good. But it should not decide. I have hired good people with bad CVs.

**Interviewer:** If you could change one thing, what would it be?

**Hiring Manager:** Understand the site before you send people. Come to Dammam. Walk the warehouse. Then you will not need me to explain on the phone.

**Interviewer:** And on your side?

**Hiring Manager:** *[Pause.]* Maybe answer faster. And maybe tell HR when I change something. My HR partner will say I never tell her. She is probably right. Okay, I am at the Jubail gate. I have to go. Ma'a salama.
