# Interview: Senior Recruiter

- Role: Senior Recruiter
- Office: Riyadh
- Date: 6 October 2026
- Interviewer: Riklr FDE

---

**Senior Recruiter:** Can you hear me? The connection in this room is bad. Let me move closer to the router. Okay. Better?

**Interviewer:** Much better. Thanks for joining. How long have you been with the firm?

**Senior Recruiter:** Six years. I started in Dubai and moved to Riyadh when we opened here. I cover logistics mostly, some construction.

**Interviewer:** How many roles are you working right now?

**Senior Recruiter:** Open right now, maybe fourteen. Wait, let me check my tracker. *[Typing.]* Fifteen. One came in this morning, I have not even called the manager back. Over the year, a lot more. I do not count anymore. Most of the logistics group work comes to me because I have known their people for years.

**Interviewer:** Is that your own tracker or the Monday sheet?

**Senior Recruiter:** My own. A spreadsheet. Role, client, manager's mobile, date I sent the shortlist, date I chased, what they said. The Monday sheet only has the dates. Mine has the real story.

**Interviewer:** Walk me through a typical role from the start.

**Senior Recruiter:** The hiring manager sends a message, sometimes email, sometimes on the messaging app. "I need a fleet manager." I call them back when I can. Sometimes same day, sometimes two days later because I am on other calls. We talk for fifteen minutes. I fill the intake form after, from my notes.

**Interviewer:** What do you ask in that call?

**Senior Recruiter:** Experience, skills, salary if they will tell me. Many do not want to give a band. They say "send me good people and we will see." Then I write the job description, usually from a previous one for a similar role. Send it to the hiring manager to approve. That can take a few days. They are busy. Sometimes they change things, send it back, I change it again.

**Interviewer:** Then the job goes live?

**Senior Recruiter:** Yes. I post it on the two boards and the professional network. I ask a sourcer to search our database and the network. Then I wait for applications to come in and start sourcing myself. I have saved searches on the network for the profiles I hire all the time. Fleet, warehouse, transport planning. Every morning I check what is new.

**Interviewer:** Which part takes longest for you?

**Senior Recruiter:** Sourcing. Finding the right people. Especially Saudis with logistics experience. Everyone wants them. The good ones are not on job boards. You have to find them on the network, call them, convince them. That takes days. For an expat role, easy, I have hundreds of CVs. For a Saudi role, I can spend a week and find three people.

**Interviewer:** And screening? The Managing Partner said recruiters are drowning in CVs.

**Senior Recruiter:** Yes and no. There are a lot of CVs. But most of them I can reject in ten seconds. Wrong country, no experience in the sector, a cook applying for a fleet manager. The ones that take time are the good ones, because I call them. I would not want a machine deciding who I call. But if it took away the obvious ones, fine.

**Interviewer:** How long do those calls take?

**Senior Recruiter:** Most of my afternoons. Twenty minutes, half an hour each, sometimes more if the candidate likes to talk. In Ramadan it moves to the evening. Nobody wants a call at two in the afternoon when they are fasting. So I do them after iftar, from home, with the kids in the background.

**Interviewer:** How many of them go somewhere?

**Senior Recruiter:** Maybe half. The other half end on salary, notice period or nationality. He wants double the band, or he has ninety days notice, or he is not Saudi and the role needs a Saudi. Sometimes I find that out in the first two minutes and then I am polite for another twenty. I wish I knew those things before I picked up the phone.

**Interviewer:** When did that last happen?

**Senior Recruiter:** Yesterday. A transport planner. Good CV, ten years, all in the Eastern Province. First question, I ask his notice. Ninety days and his company does not buy out. The client wants someone before the new contract starts in November. Khalas, finished.

**Interviewer:** Is there a visa side to that as well?

**Senior Recruiter:** For expats, yes. Is his iqama transferable, will his sponsor release him, is he in the country at all. Last month I had a Jordanian candidate at final interview. The client loved him. Then we found out his current employer would not release him for six months. Nobody asked at the start because the role was "open nationality" on my form. Then HR said it should have been Saudi anyway. So two problems in one candidate.

**Interviewer:** How do you know whether a role needs a Saudi national?

**Senior Recruiter:** Sometimes the hiring manager says. Sometimes HR at the client says it later. The problem is the hiring manager often does not know. HR knows the Saudisation numbers. The hiring manager just wants someone good. So I send a shortlist with two Indians, a Jordanian, a Filipino and one Saudi, and HR comes back and says "we can only take Saudis for this grade." And I start again.

**Interviewer:** How often does that happen?

**Senior Recruiter:** Too often. I should ask every time. The form just says "nationality preference" and I usually put "open" unless they tell me. Honestly sometimes I leave it blank. I made my own list of questions for intake calls, one page. But when I have fifteen roles I do not always use it.

*[A phone rings in the background.]*

**Senior Recruiter:** Sorry, this is the logistics group. I have to take it. One minute.

*[Partly in Arabic.]*

**Senior Recruiter:** Hala, ya ustaz. Yes. Yes, I sent it Sunday. Five CVs. The second one? He is available in thirty days. Okay. You want to add customs experience? For this role? Okay, okay. Send me on the app, I will check. Ma'a salama.

*[Back.]*

**Senior Recruiter:** You see? That was the hiring manager for a transport supervisor. I sent the shortlist two days ago. Now he wants customs experience. That was not in the intake. That was not in the job description he approved. So now I check which of my five have customs, probably one. Then I go back to sourcing.

**Interviewer:** Will you log that change anywhere?

**Senior Recruiter:** In my tracker. Maybe in the ATS comment if I remember. He told me on the phone, so there is no email. HR will not know unless I tell them.

**Interviewer:** Tell me about the logistics group more generally.

**Senior Recruiter:** They are a good client. They pay well. But they change. I do the intake, everything is clear, they approve the job description. I send five people. Then the hiring manager says "actually we need someone with warehouse system experience" or "we merged two roles." Or the hiring manager leaves and the new one wants something different. I have had roles go four rounds with them. Each round is another two weeks.

**Interviewer:** How fast do they reply to a shortlist?

**Senior Recruiter:** Slow. They say they will reply in two or three days. It is usually two weeks. I chase on the messaging app. Sometimes the hiring manager is travelling between sites. Sometimes they are waiting for their director. Meanwhile my candidates are calling me asking for news. I have a group on the messaging app with some of the regular candidates, the ones I place again and again. They message me every day. "Any news, akhi?" I have nothing to tell them.

**Interviewer:** How do you send the shortlist itself?

**Senior Recruiter:** Email. I have my own template, a table with name, current role, salary, notice, nationality and my comments. I copy the CVs in as attachments. The managers do not open the ATS format.

**Interviewer:** Do you log the reason when a shortlist is rejected?

**Senior Recruiter:** I try. The ATS dropdown has a few options but they never fit. "Requirements changed" is not an option, so I pick "other." I paste the email in the comment box.

**Interviewer:** You mentioned Arabic CVs earlier. How do you handle them?

**Senior Recruiter:** The ATS reads English CVs fine. Arabic ones come through as a mess. The name ends up in the wrong field, or blank. Sometimes the letters are reversed. So I open the file and read it myself. I can, I read Arabic. But the Dubai team cannot, and the sourcers in Dubai skip those CVs. That is a problem because many good Saudi candidates write their CV in Arabic.

**Interviewer:** What about duplicates?

**Senior Recruiter:** All the time. Mohammed Al-Otaibi applies through the Saudi job board. Then the sourcer finds him on the network as Muhammad Alotaibi. Then he is in the database from 2022 with a different email. Three records. Once I sent the same man twice in one shortlist under two spellings. The client noticed. That was embarrassing. The HR lady called me and said, "Is he twins?" I wanted to disappear.

**Interviewer:** If you could change one thing, what would it be?

**Senior Recruiter:** Fewer roles per person. I cannot do a good intake when I have fourteen, fifteen open roles. And clients who reply. If a client replied in three days like the contract says, I would hit the SLA most of the time.

**Interviewer:** And if the firm brought in AI screening?

**Senior Recruiter:** If it reads Arabic and it saves me time, fine. But my time goes into calls, chasing clients and finding Saudis. Not reading CVs.

**Interviewer:** Thank you. This is very useful.

**Senior Recruiter:** You are welcome. Please tell them about the logistics group.
