# Interview: Compliance and Data Officer

- Role: Compliance and Data Officer
- Office: Dubai
- Date: 8 October 2026
- Interviewer: Riklr FDE

---

**Compliance and Data Officer:** Come in. Ignore the boxes, those are client contracts for the archive. We are finally scanning everything from before 2020. Half of it nobody has looked at in years.

**Interviewer:** Thanks for meeting. What does your role cover?

**Compliance and Data Officer:** Data protection across all three offices. Client contracts. Candidate consent. Any time we use a new tool that touches candidate data, I review it. I joined eighteen months ago. Before me nobody owned this. Finance did some of it, the office manager did some of it. Mostly nobody did it.

**Interviewer:** Which data protection rules apply to the firm?

**Compliance and Data Officer:** In the UAE, the federal personal data protection law. In Saudi Arabia, the Personal Data Protection Law, which is now actively enforced. Qatar has its own law too. They are not identical, but the basics are similar. You need a lawful basis to hold someone's data, usually consent for us. You use it only for the purpose they agreed to. You keep it only as long as you need it. You delete it when they ask.

**Interviewer:** What is the firm's policy on retention?

**Compliance and Data Officer:** I wrote it last year. We keep a candidate record for twenty-four months from their last activity with us. Activity means they applied, updated their profile or confirmed consent. After that we either get fresh consent or delete. That is the policy.

**Interviewer:** And in practice?

**Compliance and Data Officer:** In practice the ATS still holds records from 2019. The deletion job was never switched on. I raised it twice. Each time someone said the old database is our biggest asset and we should not delete it.

**Interviewer:** Who said that?

**Compliance and Data Officer:** The first time, the Managing Partner. The second time, two of the senior recruiters in the same meeting. I understand them. Their placements come from that database. But "it is useful" is not a lawful basis. I said that in the meeting. It was not my most popular day.

**Interviewer:** What about consent?

**Compliance and Data Officer:** New applications through the boards and our careers page carry a consent checkbox, so those are fine. Older records were imported from spreadsheets before we had the ATS. Many have no consent recorded at all. Some have consent recorded as "no" because the candidate unticked the box for future roles. Those people should not be contacted for other roles.

**Interviewer:** Do sourcers know that?

**Compliance and Data Officer:** They should. I did a training session. I am not sure everyone attended. It was in the week before Eid, which, looking back, was not the best timing. Half the Riyadh office had already left. When a recruiter pulls someone from the database and attaches them to a new role, the ATS does not update the activity date. So a record can look old and unused, but it is in a shortlist we sent last month.

**Interviewer:** Has that caused issues?

**Compliance and Data Officer:** One complaint this year from a candidate in Riyadh who got a call about a role and asked how we had his CV. We handled it. But under the Saudi law that could have been serious. I am worried about scale. We send shortlists with full CVs to clients by email. That is a transfer of personal data to a third party.

**Interviewer:** How did you handle the complaint?

**Compliance and Data Officer:** He asked us to delete everything. I found his record and deleted it. Two weeks later a sourcer in Dubai attached him to a different role. Same man, different spelling, a second record from an older import. He called again, much less polite. I had to apologise in writing. Then I searched by phone number and found a third record. So, three records, one person.

**Interviewer:** Where else does candidate data sit, outside the ATS?

**Compliance and Data Officer:** Everywhere. *[Sighs.]* Recruiters keep their own spreadsheets on their laptops with names, salaries and mobile numbers. They have groups on the messaging app with candidates. Shortlists go out as email with the CVs attached, so every client inbox has a copy. Some recruiters save CVs to their desktop to build their own shortlist document. The Managing Partner forwards CVs from his phone. The policy covers all of that. Enforcing it is another thing.

**Interviewer:** Have you raised that?

**Compliance and Data Officer:** I put it in my quarterly report. The response was, "Let us not slow the recruiters down." There is always a role that is late, so there is never a good week to fix it.

**Interviewer:** The Managing Partner wants AI to screen CVs and search the database. What is your view?

**Compliance and Data Officer:** I am not against it. But I have three conditions. First, it only touches records we are allowed to use. That means consent recorded and inside retention. Second, a human makes every decision about a candidate. The tool can sort and suggest. It cannot reject someone on its own. Third, we can explain to a candidate or a client why the tool scored someone the way it did.

**Interviewer:** Why those three?

**Compliance and Data Officer:** The first is the law here. The second and third are where the world is going. In Europe, the AI Act treats hiring tools as high risk. In New York, employers using automated tools have to publish bias audits. Neither applies to us directly. But some of our clients are global companies, and their own policies follow those standards. One bank client already asked us in a tender whether we use automated decision-making on candidates.

**Interviewer:** What did you answer?

**Compliance and Data Officer:** We said no, because we do not. If we start, I need to be able to show it is fair. That means testing outcomes by gender and nationality at minimum.

**Interviewer:** Do you know whether current screening, done by people, is fair on those dimensions?

**Compliance and Data Officer:** I have never measured it. I would like to. I have heard concerns from junior staff that some roles get almost no women shortlisted. If we train a tool on past decisions, we will copy whatever is there.

**Interviewer:** Why has it not been measured?

**Compliance and Data Officer:** Honestly, nobody asked for it, and I do not have the data skills. I can read a contract. I cannot build a report from the ATS export. I asked for an analyst last budget round. We hired another recruiter instead.

*[Her phone lights up. She glances at it and turns it face down.]*

**Compliance and Data Officer:** Finance. It can wait.

**Interviewer:** Does the firm hold sensitive data?

**Compliance and Data Officer:** Nationality, gender, sometimes religion or marital status if the candidate puts it in their CV, which many do in this region. Photos too. Date of birth, sometimes in the Hijri calendar on Saudi CVs. Salary expectations. Visa status. None of that should go to a third-party AI service without a contract that covers where the data is stored and processed. Saudi data leaving the kingdom has its own rules.

**Interviewer:** Where is the ATS hosted?

**Compliance and Data Officer:** It is a cloud product. The vendor says data sits in a European region. I have asked about a local region. No answer yet. I chased them again last week. Their account manager is very friendly and says nothing.

**Interviewer:** What would you want to see from any prototype?

**Compliance and Data Officer:** It should show me which records it excluded and why. It should log every suggestion it made and what the recruiter did with it. And it should never show a candidate's photo, religion or marital status to the model.

**Interviewer:** Anything else we should know?

**Compliance and Data Officer:** The duplicate problem matters for compliance too. If someone asks us to delete their data and they exist under three spellings, we delete one and keep two. That is a breach. You heard what happened with the Riyadh candidate. I do not want a second one.

**Interviewer:** Thank you. That is a clear set of conditions.

**Compliance and Data Officer:** Please put them in writing for the Managing Partner. He listens more when it comes from outside. And if you need the consent field definitions, I have them somewhere in these boxes. Or on the shared drive. Probably the shared drive.
